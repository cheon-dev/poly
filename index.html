What Are Polysemous Words?

Polysemy is a linguistic phenomenon in which one word has multiple related meanings. These meanings are connected through a shared idea or concept, but the specific meaning depends on how the word is used in a sentence, passage, or literary text.

A polysemous word may be used literally in literary texts. Writers use these words to express ideas, describe characters, establish settings, and communicate emotions. Therefore, understanding the meaning of a polysemous word requires more than knowing its definition; readers must also examine how the word is used in the context.

Examples of Polysemous Words

BRANCH Tree / Lineage

A part of a tree; a division of an organization or group.

"A raven rested on a branch of the ancient tree."

"The family belonged to a distant branch of the royal lineage."

BRIGHT Light / Intellect

Giving off or reflecting much light; intelligent.

"The bright moon illuminated the dark forest."

"The bright young scholar solved the king's difficult riddle."

SHARP Edge / Tone

Having a cutting edge; harsh or sudden in tone.

"The warrior carried a sharp sword into battle."

"The old man gave a sharp reply to the stranger."

DEEP Depth / Emotion

Extending far downward; intense or profound in feeling.

"The river was deep near the cliffs."

"The poem expressed the character's deep sorrow."

POINT Tip / Idea

The tip of an object; an idea or statement.

"The point of the spear glistened beneath the moonlight."

"The narrator made an important point about human nature."

Part 3. Distinguishing Related Meanings

Distinguishing Related Meanings

A. Steps

Words with multiple meanings may have similar or related ideas, making them difficult to distinguish. To understand a polysemous word correctly, it is important to examine how it is used and identify the meaning that best matches the given sentence or literary passage.

Follow these simple steps to help you recognize, compare, and distinguish the different meanings of polysemous words. Each step will guide you in choosing the most appropriate meaning based on the context.

1. Identify Identify the polysemous word.
2. List List the possible meanings of the word.
3. Read the Sentence or Passage Read the entire sentence or passage.
4. Look for Context Clues Look for context clues.
5. Compare Compare the possible meanings.
6. Eliminate Eliminate meanings that do not fit.
7. Choose Choose the meaning that best fits the literary context.
8. Explain Explain why the chosen meaning is appropriate.

Examples

BEARING

Carrying a physical object; enduring a difficult responsibility or emotional burden.

Carrying: "The porter was bearing a heavy box across the courtyard."

Enduring: "The protagonist continued bearing the burden of his family's shame."

RESOLUTION

The solution or conclusion of a conflict; the determination to achieve a goal.

Conclusion: "The novel's resolution revealed the truth behind the family conflict."

Determination: "Her resolution to finish her studies remained strong despite the difficulties."

RESERVE

A supply kept for future use; caution or restraint in expressing feelings.

Supply: "The villagers kept a reserve of food for the winter."

Restraint: "The young woman spoke with reserve when asked about her past."

YIELD

To produce crops or results; to surrender or give in.

Produce: "The farm is expected to yield a large harvest this year."

Surrender: "The young man finally yielded to his family's pressure."

TEMPER

To moderate or soften a quality; to strengthen a material through heating.

Moderate: "Time helped temper his anger and made him more patient."

Strengthen: "The blacksmith heated the metal to temper it and improve its strength."

Part 4. Strategies for Interpreting Polysemous Words

The 5-Step Meaning Check

Understanding words with multiple meanings requires careful reading and analysis. Sometimes, identifying the correct meaning can be challenging, especially when a word has several related meanings. Using simple strategies can help readers examine the word, understand its usage, and determine the meaning that best fits the literary text. The following five steps will serve as a guide in interpreting polysemous words and improving your understanding of literary passages.

1

Read the context

Read the entire sentence or passage to understand the situation.

2

Identify possible meanings

Think of the different meanings of the underlined word.

3

Use prior knowledge

Connect the word to what you already know about English, literature, and the topic.

4

Check definitions

Consult a dictionary to verify the possible meanings.

5

Eliminate and select

Remove meanings that do not fit the context and choose the most appropriate interpretation.
